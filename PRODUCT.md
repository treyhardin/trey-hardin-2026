# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro, Cloudflare Pages

## Users

**Primary:** Agency owners, CEOs, and brand founders evaluating a senior designer for partnership or hire. They're time-poor, scanning for credibility and fit before reaching out.

**Secondary:** Other designers and creative directors looking for inspiration or benchmarking work.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio site that demonstrates Trey Hardin's design leadership and drives qualified inbound engagement from decision-makers at agencies, brands, and studios. Success means a visitor leaves convinced of senior-level capability and takes a concrete next step (contact, collaboration inquiry, speaking engagement).

## Positioning

Breadth of experience across the world's largest ecommerce brands. Interdisciplinary expertise that informs creative process. 10+ years leading creative teams in digital design and ecommerce. The ability to bridge the gap between design, engineering, and brand stakeholders — not just a pixel pusher, but a strategic partner who speaks every language in the room.

- No studio name — personal brand only: "Trey Hardin"
- Design direction/visual system to be defined later
- Specific client list and case study content to be provided later
- Site starts as a landing page, expands incrementally

## Evidence on Hand

- No client logos, testimonials, or case study content yet — future work must not fabricate these
- Case studies and client list will be provided by the user

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.x AA compliance required.

## Product Principles

1. **Substance over decoration** — credibility comes from what you've done, not how flashy the page looks.
2. **Senior-level signal** — every element should communicate experience, judgment, and leadership, not junior enthusiasm.
3. **Bridge, don't silo** — the site itself should demonstrate the ability to connect design thinking with technical execution and business outcomes.
4. **Respect the visitor's time** — agency owners and CEOs scan fast. Information architecture and hierarchy matter more than scroll length.
