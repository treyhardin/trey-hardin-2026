Why Aeonik Pro

Aeonik is the modern successor to the neo-grotesque tradition — designed by the team behind FF Meta and FF DIN. It carries the same clean authority but feels current, not archival. The 25-weight range gives you enormous typographic control across headings, body, and captions without switching families.

For a portfolio that needs to signal seniority without shouting, Aeonik Pro hits the register between Inter (too generic) and Agrandir (too expressive). It's the kind of typeface that says "I know what I'm doing" without trying.

Pairing: Keep IBM Plex Mono for code/labels. Aeonik handles everything else.

Why Helvetica Neue

The original neo-grotesque benchmark. Helvetica Neue isn't a safe choice — it's a statement. Using it on a portfolio signals you understand typography deeply enough to work with the font everyone knows but few use well.

The 41-weight library includes condensed variants, giving you tight, impactful headings without going to a separate display family. The UltraLight through Black range covers every role.

Pairing: IBM Plex Mono stays. Helvetica Neue carries the entire visual weight of the site — no second display font needed.

Why Suisse Intl

Suisse is the font that every design tool chose for its own interface type. It's the neo-grotesque that feels like it was designed for screens — tight, precise, with a slightly narrower cut that packs more words per line without sacrificing readability.

For a portfolio that lives on the web, Suisse Intl's screen-optimized geometry is a practical advantage. The "Intl" version adds extended language support and slightly wider proportions that work better at small sizes.

Pairing: IBM Plex Mono for code. Suisse Intl handles body and headings with equal authority.

Current: Inter + IBM Plex Mono — functional but generic. All three recommendations elevate the typographic voice while keeping the same clean, text-forward layout.
